## Resolutions and rationale

### Experience, not form placement

The differentiator is observable cause and effect: personal statements affect layout, functions, equipment or materials, and a requested change affects that same concept. Delaying contact collection alone does not meet the brief. A generic image with personalized text is insufficient.

The visitor sees a personal functional concept, may revise it, and can then choose advisor continuation with a selected project. First-result access and refinement remain independent of contact fields. The intended business outcome is a voluntary qualified lead/appointment grounded in a useful design discussion.

### Correct the timing interpretation

Initial Conversation and some role responses budgeted two to three minutes for discovery alone. The coordinator challenged that interpretation against the complete product promise; Product, Conversation, Interaction, Parametric, Research and QA corrected it. The primary measurement runs from conversation start to the first visible personalized furnished view, including visitor pauses, planning and rendering. Subintervals diagnose delays. No measured result currently supports the target. Three to five subsequent revisions are outside this first-result interval.

### One adaptive discovery, two geometry states

Unknown measurements remain unknown. With user room information, use “Konzept auf Grundlage Ihrer Raumangaben”; actual checks name their exact scope. Without sufficient geometry, show “Ihre Wünsche in einem Beispielraum · Raummaße noch offen”, with local uncertainty on decisions such as an island. An unknown room still permits functional and material personalization; actual fit to the visitor's room is not evaluable.

The next question addresses the largest current design risk. Exact water coordinates are not a universal entry gate, but a proposed changed sink wall may require clarification. An opening being irrelevant to the first furniture wall can justify deferring its precise dimensions. One decisive additional question is a simple-path target, not a hard cap that overrides conflicts. Likewise, selective visual aids are a principle, not a rigid quota.

### Product/QA wording disagreement resolved

Product initially suggested “anhand Ihrer Angaben räumlich geprüft”. QA objected that this could imply broader professional verification. Product explicitly withdrew it after direct challenge. The integrated draft uses the limited wording above; no blanket “spatially verified” claim remains.

### Start empty; reveal actual work

The shared workspace starts unfurnished. An optional example before entry is clearly marked as an example. A room outline or loading state does not count as the first concept. Known room facts and material samples may appear progressively. Perspective and plan refer to the same project/version. Mobile behavior must be tested; a permanent workspace must not crowd the conversation.

### Recommended first rendering approach

Use an inviting model-based 3D perspective, with the same geometry and unit IDs as the on-demand plan and provisional furniture list. Fixed furniture dimensions survive room adaptation. A free image model is not an authoritative geometry or SKU source. The team's initial recommendation does not make photorealism a demonstrator acceptance gate; the user's desired emotional standard remains a discussion point and requires participant evaluation.

### Templates and a synthetic case

Recommend six to eight curated variants from a small set of layout families, extending toward the user's 20–30 defaults when coverage evidence supports it. A count alone is not acceptance. The coordinator uses line, parallel lines, L and U as working family labels, with conditional island/peninsula variants; the taxonomy has no product guarantee.

Kitchen and Parametric discussed one explicitly synthetic 400×500cm room, south open, north window wall left unfurnished, west sink zone. Eight generic fixed-width units provide a shared family-use example and a functional island-to-east-workline change. Exact window/connection positions and appliance assumptions are demo setup, not facts extracted from the abbreviated visitor dialogue. Model geometry is not proof of installation, appliance clearance, services or source-authorized articles. A second no-measurements case is mandatory to avoid proving only a prepared ideal scene.

### Proposed change to the demonstrator dependency

Earlier planning made a source-verified GL subset a prerequisite for the whole demonstrator. Product, Kitchen, Parametric and QA recommend decoupling a generic fixed-size furniture experience from a separate source-verified Ballerina article-mapping sample. The sample must not activate a catalog or imply other furniture has verified SKUs. Existing catalog authorization/readiness gates remain intact.

This is a proposal for the user's discussion, not a silently approved replacement of the previous demo scope. The integrated draft identifies the difference. CARAT, shared-catalog deployment and commercial use remain separate workstreams.

### Evaluation

Three traceable wish-to-decision links belong in suitable test cases, not in a mandatory three-wish interview. A functional revision must be demonstrable; every visitor need not perform one. Participants should be able to locate their main functional benefit themselves, and Kitchen should independently confirm it in the model.

The proposed first qualitative pilot has approximately twelve participants. Measure first-result time, correction behavior, understood assumptions and usefulness before optional contact. Record overall wish fit separately from must-haves, style, function and spatial plausibility. A 0–10 response is a subjective rating, not a technical accuracy percentage. A small pilot does not certify the 70–80% hypothesis.

The proposed 1,000 synthetic cases evaluate coverage, constraints, missing/conflicting data, stable revisions and structured exports. They do not establish customer satisfaction or automatic model training. Selection/ranking improvement needs explicit reviewed feedback and independent evaluation.

## Remaining tensions, not manufactured consensus

- An explicit example-room label may reduce personal identification; hiding uncertainty is not an acceptable remedy. Test both comprehension and emotional value.
- Fast discovery leaves more assumptions; complete measurement would undermine the short conversation. Test adaptive paths and the usefulness of a personal provisional concept.
- A consistent 3D scene may be less emotionally compelling than photorealism. Compare approaches without sacrificing geometry, editing or honest product status.
- Six to eight variants may have insufficient real coverage. Add variants based on missed cases rather than count targets.
