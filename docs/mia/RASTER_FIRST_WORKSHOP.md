# Raster-first concept workshop

Date: 2026-10-15. Active area: mein-kuechenexperte.de. Status: new user-directed alternative for team discussion, not an implementation or runtime acceptance.

## Current user instruction

Discuss a different approach: a conversation lasting at most two minutes, without using binding room measurements, then a realistic initial kitchen realization based on the visitor's statements in a room built like a grid. Discuss details with the visitor after they see the idea. The user asks whether this approach is understood.

This instruction changes the starting point of EXPERIENCE_DRAFT_DE.md. Do not reintroduce the old mandatory question about rough dimensions, a floor plan, window offsets or water position before the first idea. The grid room is the deliberate starting environment for everybody, not only a fallback for users missing measurements.

Immediate user clarification: the kitchen is a draft based on the visitor's wishes, not free planning. Treat visitor requirements as controlling the proposal; do not invent an autonomous kitchen or expose a blank CAD editor. The earlier prepared-template proposal provides the bounded starting point to assess. The grid is a presentation/placement aid, not permission to design arbitrarily. Details and actual room adaptation come after this wish-driven first draft.

## Shared interpretation to challenge

- Stage 1 designs the kitchen's use, atmosphere, furniture and arrangement in a neutral grid-based scene. It does not fit furniture to the visitor's measured dwelling.
- The grid describes coordinates/modules and consistent object proportions. Furniture has actual defined sizes. It is not an instruction to stretch cabinets with a scaled room, and no universal manufacturer grid size has been established.
- Ask only the few personal/functional/visual questions whose answers can materially change the first concept. Skip supplied information. Voice/text, selective image assistance, empathy and natural conversational continuity remain requirements.
- The user explicitly caps the conversation at 120 seconds. Separate conversation duration from end-to-end time to first visible furnished idea. Do not silently relabel rendering wait as conversation or imply a measured two-minute visual guarantee. Recommend how to make first visibility early while preserving truthful timing.
- The first scene must feel like a kitchen the visitor could want, with specific functions and coherent proportions. It cannot claim actual room fit. Avoid wrapping the experience in repeated disclaimers; agree concise visible language.
- Stage 2 uses the visible concept to discuss changes, furniture details and, when useful, actual room geometry. Numeric data and fixed constraints are introduced deliberately rather than guessed during stage 1.
- Later adaptation may make an island/layout impossible. Preserve priorities and furniture identity where possible, compare versions and discuss alternatives; do not silently rescale furniture or promise every initial layout will fit.
- Generic fixed-size demo furniture and verified manufacturer article assignments remain distinct. Catalog activation, paid calls, production operations and CARAT import remain out of scope.

## Team task

Seven roles reuse the original common workshop context. Each returns a short role-specific recommendation and strongest objection, then directly challenges at least one peer. Coordinator integrates actual agreements and dissent into a concise German alternative draft. Independent QA reviews the integrated result. No implementation or presentation refresh is commissioned by this discussion alone. Do not edit files as a specialist.

Resolve: minimal questions, grid scene versus real room, 120-second stop behavior, realistic rendering versus proven feasibility, template/module strategy, and the transition from first idea to subsequent detail work without restarting the entire conversation.

## Actual discussion results

All seven existing specialists read the new shared brief and returned updated recommendations. Direct exchanges included Product/Conversation/Research, Conversation/Interaction, Interaction/Parametric, Parametric/Kitchen, Kitchen/Research and Research/QA. The coordinator integrated the outcome into [the German alternative draft](RASTER_FIRST_DRAFT_DE.md).

Agreements:

- Raster-first is the default for every visitor, not an unknown-measurement fallback. No pre-result room, window or water questions, including supposedly decisive exceptions.
- Visitor wishes drive bounded selection/customization of prepared layouts. It is not a blank CAD scene or unconstrained generation. Layout preference may be supplied; otherwise the selected family is a changeable proposal.
- Four useful preference fields can usually be elicited through three conversational openings: everyday use/improvement, must-haves/retained equipment, atmosphere. Already supplied answers eliminate questions. This is not a mandatory questionnaire.
- Use a coherent furnished concept with fixed object sizes. Scene extent, camera, grid and lighting are presentation data, not assumed customer-room measurements. No universal 60cm grid is asserted.
- Prefer “Ihre Küchenidee im Beispielraum” for visitors, plus one understandable statement that real-room adaptation comes later. The grid can remain subtle and become clearer on demand.
- Visitor reaction and material/function changes may precede room adaptation. Later spatial conflicts require explained alternatives and version comparison. Preserve wishes and hard keep-existing-object requirements. Preserve furniture identity only for unchanged objects; replacements have linked new identities.
- The 120-second conversation interval includes both speakers, pauses and interaction. Separately record render wait and complete start-to-visible-result duration. No hidden unlimited wait or relabelled stage may conceal a missed target.

Unresolved edge case: uninterrupted long visitor speech conflicts with an unconditional 120-second stop unless MIA interrupts. The recommendation to ask no new question after about 90 seconds helps but does not prove the cap. Specialists preferred a gentle early transition; no user authorization for a hard audio cut was inferred. The integrated draft preserves the requirement and exposes this execution decision rather than pretending consensus resolved it.

Research proposes a small qualitative first check with six varied participants, including an island wish that later cannot fit. This explores comprehension, actual personalization and disappointment after room adaptation; it does not certify timing, the 70–80% match hypothesis, or real-world planning correctness.

Product and independent QA read the complete integrated alternative and accepted it for discussion without blocking findings. QA's suggestion to state the full text alternative explicitly was applied. Both preserved the 120-second requirement and the unresolved long-speech case. This is acceptance of a discussion document, not a working product or measured timing.

No implementation, presentation rewrite, provider call, catalog change or runtime test was performed. Existing source grants, CRM ownership and tenant boundaries are unchanged. Documentation link/JSON and whitespace checks are the proportionate delivery checks; earlier runtime tests are not new evidence for this alternative.
