# MIA product experience workshop — shared briefing

Date: 2026-10-15. Active area: mein-kuechenexperte.de. Scope: a coordinated senior-specialist discussion and a first integrated product draft for the user to discuss. No runtime implementation, paid provider calls, production writes, catalog activation, or deployment in this workshop.

## Why this workshop exists

The coordinator previously answered individual observations in isolation and reduced the differentiation problem to the timing of contact collection. The user rejected that response. This workshop must integrate all supplied requirements into one customer experience, with explicit tradeoffs and a concrete example, rather than repeat a generic journey or produce seven disconnected opinions.

## Shared product intent and user evidence

- MIA is an explicitly selected voice-first AI kitchen concept assistant for mein-kuechenexperte.de, with a complete text alternative. KEA remains distinct and Anna unchanged.
- A natural discovery conversation should take approximately two to three minutes. This is a hypothesis to test, not an achieved performance guarantee. Clarify whether first rendering and later revisions fit within or follow that interval.
- The visitor should immediately receive a personalized visible kitchen concept during the experience. Target perceived fit is 70–80%, to be assessed across style, function, explicit wishes, room plausibility and subjective satisfaction. A finished technical kitchen plan is not the initial deliverable.
- The visitor must understand the benefit from entry. The desired differentiation is a useful, personal experience, not a generic picture questionnaire followed by sales calls. Lead generation is a legitimate business goal. Do not equate good experience solely with postponing a form.
- The conversation must flow: no recited answers, running spoken summaries, or forced closing recap. Retain facts internally. Ask a relevant next question and skip information already supplied. MIA may be empathic and occasionally laugh/chuckle when appropriate; actual audio behavior remains untested.
- Selective small images help when tapping is easier than speaking. They must not turn every question into picture selection. Keep a room diagram visible when working on openings.
- User reference topics: open/closed room; kitchen line/L/U/G/island or undecided; style; light/dark/color preferences; worktop; oven height; fridge built-in/freestanding; fridge type and width; cooking alone/together/family/friends; owner/renter; renovation/new build; budget; decision maker; installation timing; postcode and first/last name/email/phone.
- Treat the images as reference topics, not a mandated full question sequence or a verified assortment. Separate openness from kitchen shape, and refrigerator integration from type and width. Do not copy stock imagery or another provider's phone number, offer count, delivery claims, or verification behavior.
- Room dimensions may be supplied or unknown. Visitor may tap walls with windows/doors and supply widths/offsets when known. Establish an unambiguous interior-facing left/right reference. Do not require detailed measuring before a first concept; explain what unknown geometry permits.
- User proposed scaling a reference room shell from 10×10×10m to the actual dimensions. Furniture must retain real fixed dimensions. Prefer numeric room geometry and deterministic furniture constraints; a visual model alone is not a source of measurements.
- User proposed 20–30 default kitchen plans using common furniture, then adaptation. Assess a smaller initial set and a path to wider coverage. These should be parametric templates with suitability constraints, not stretched pictures.
- User proposed 1,000 synthetic planning cases and feedback-driven learning. Distinguish valid test-case generation, human evaluation and ranking improvement from automatic self-training or proven customer fit.
- Later seller handoff must connect visible furniture to structured units and provisional article candidates. Do not infer exact SKUs from pixels or invent verified manufacturer availability/pricing. CARAT transfer is a later evidence-dependent integration, not a demo dependency.
- Ballerina GL/XL PDFs exist. A matching local catalog was located in CARAT-AUTOPLAN-AGENT, not proven as a shared remote SCAS service. Neither series has an active approved pointer for MIA; no verified article candidates or MIA consumer grant were established. See CATALOG_REUSE_ASSESSMENT.md. No new database or full lifecycle remediation is required to discuss the customer concept.
- Das Küchenhaus and mein-kuechenexperte are separate. Do not use tenant customer records, terms, prices or permissions across that boundary. A neutral shared catalog is still an architecture proposal.
- Original goal includes 3–5 visitor revisions, optional advisor contact/appointment and a structured CRM handoff. Contact fields are entered in a protected form; no real submission exists in the current sketch.
- Existing artifacts describe first concept/refinement before optional contact. Treat proposed details such as phone optionality, exact question order, instant anonymous resume/export and additional promises as design decisions to justify, not user-approved requirements.
- Demonstrator for Markus Kiesel should be a coherent playable experience; date remains unconfirmed and no paid budget is approved. No real generation, voice, CRM, calendar, 70–80% fit or two-to-three-minute success is currently demonstrated by the scripted sketch.

## Roles and rounds

The coordinator supplies this same briefing to seven senior roles: Product & Service Design; Conversation & Voice Experience; UX & Interaction Design; Kitchen Planning; Parametric/3D Planning Architecture; UX Research/Decision Psychology/Conversion; independent QA/Experience Evaluation.

Round 1: each role returns a concrete proposal, a strongest objection to its own proposal, dependencies, and decisions requiring team resolution. Read-only; do not edit files or start tests/providers. Prefer German findings. Cite the requirement you are resolving. Avoid external browsing unless a specific unstable factual claim is necessary; this is an internal design workshop, not a market benchmark.

Round 2: the coordinator circulates conflicting proposals. Each role critiques other roles' recommendations and answers a named question. Agents may message the other workshop agents directly after the roster is provided. Preserve genuine disagreement; do not manufacture consensus.

Integration: coordinator records decisions, rejected alternatives with reasons, unresolved choices and exact evidence limits; prepares one German customer-facing discussion draft. Independent QA reviews the integrated draft after specialist cross-review. This workshop is not acceptance of a working MIA product.

1. A distinctive product thesis and credible entry benefit.
2. A complete short journey with an actual example conversation and selective visual moments; account for visitor speech, uncertainty and rendering time.
3. Minimum needed facts, conditional/deferred topics, and handling of incomplete/conflicting room data.
4. The first-result screen: what exactly the visitor sees, what it is based on, how they change it, and how assumptions remain visible without derailing the experience.
5. A feasible template/geometry/article strategy tied to seller handoff.
6. A voluntary advisor transition with a real purpose and recipient; no invented provider services.
7. A demonstrator scope and practical evaluation plan; no untested timing/fit claims.
8. A small set of meaningful choices for the user, accompanied by team recommendations.
